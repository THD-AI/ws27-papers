# 2 · A bigger grid helps when each qubit is below the threshold

Why correct errors at all? A well-known estimate for breaking today's internet encryption assumes hardware that slips about once in a thousand operations, and a calculation of two to three billion costly steps. That is millions of slips where one is enough.

The course named the dilemma: correction needs more qubits, and every added qubit decoheres too. The way out is the threshold. Below it, a bigger grid protects better. Above it, a bigger grid is worse. The factor by which the error falls per step is called lambda. In 2023 Google's distance 5 only just beat distance 3. In 2024 the error about halved at each step.

**A bigger grid helps when each qubit is below the threshold.**
