# 2 · One uncorrected slip can spoil the answer

Why correct errors at all? A well-known estimate for breaking today's internet encryption assumes hardware that slips about once in a thousand operations, and a calculation of two to three billion costly steps. That is millions of slips where one is enough.

The course named the dilemma: correction needs more qubits, and every added qubit decoheres too. If the physical qubits are reliable enough, making the grid bigger still helps. We call that the threshold. First, let's see what goes into one of those grids.
