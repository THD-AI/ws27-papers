# Loop 1: three directions for the deck

The talk, the content, the numbers and the timing are fixed (dotUni `talks/innovation/storyline.md`, `slides.md`). What is open is the visual idea that carries sixteen slides on a projector, where nothing moves and each page is a still. Read `LESSONS.md` first.

Materials we have: his own line drawing (his LinkedIn banner, the reference for any generated drawing), the hero house generated from it, the forge (OpenAI image edits, always with his hand plus a real reference, transparent single objects, no letters or numbers), the article's data with sources, his three typefaces, one green for the claim.

## A. One street

The whole talk happens in one place, drawn in his line: a street with an American timber house at one end and a German brick building at the other. Every slide is a view of that street, the way a site walk goes from one spot to the next:
- Opening: the full street, the two buildings far apart, the question.
- The American result: in front of the timber house; the 40 and the 32 counted as boards in its wall (100 boards, 32 marked).
- The picture: the two walls side by side, cut open (the drafting convention: cable dashed inside, the duct on the face, the plastered channel).
- Repainting: a building with fresh paint on the front and the old wiring behind it, the two cases on a timeline drawn as the painter's ladder.
- Germany's three bricks: we walk up to the brick building; each challenge is one brick pulled out and turned to its face, with its number counted in bricks (4,000 positions a year as bricks leaving a wall; 79%; 72%).
- The plan: scaffolding goes up on the brick building, one level per step, eight levels; the active level carries its measure.
- The close: the renovated building with the cable inside, the street again.
Generated sprites: timber house, brick building, a single brick, a duct, a channel chisel, a trowel, a ring of keys, a cable reel, scaffolding levels, a ladder. Everything exact (numbers, labels, counts) is vector on top.

## B. The survey file

Each slide is one sheet of the survey a civil engineer would make before renovating: the detail drawn large on the left in his line (the junction box with the agent, the wall section, the works council's approval stamp as a drawn object), measured findings on the right as an engineer's annotations with leader lines, and a small title block at the bottom right naming the sheet ("Survey 3: the number") and the source. The deck reads like a professional dossier; the examiner, an engineer, recognises the form.

## C. One picture a beat

Thirteen full-bleed drawings in his line, one per beat, generated from his hand with the real object as reference (the two walls, a ring of keys on a desk, a painted façade, three bricks on a table, scaffolding), each with one sentence and at most one number set large. The words are said; the slide is a picture to remember.

## Questions for the critics

Which direction (or which combination) gives the room the clearest explanation, is unique without being a gimmick, holds up with this examiner, survives a projector in a lit hall, and won't read as machine-made? What would make it ten times better than a good assertion–evidence deck?

## The pick, after two pairs of critics (reports in dotUni `talks/innovation/readers/concept-1-*.md`)

Both pairs ranked B (the survey file) first; the designer proposed a fourth direction that keeps B's precision and fixes A and C: **one cable through the whole deck.**

- **The cable is the agent.** One cable, the same on every page, enters at the left edge and leaves at the right at the height where the next page picks it up: printed side by side, the pages are one line, like his banner. It passes what each beat is about: through the timber, onto the duct, into the plaster, along the bottom of the evidence slides.
- **Data as an engineer's drafting marks, not scenery** (B): dimension lines with ticks, extension lines, leaders to findings. Numbers are plain vector text, never counted in bricks or boards (the examiner's risk 1).
- **The claim is on the page from the first click** (lesson 1); clicks add evidence.
- **Restraint** (C): one large drawing or one measured figure per page, never both crowded; the scale changes from page to page (detail, section, object, an almost empty page with only the cable).
- **Generated drawings only where a real object helps** (the opening and closing house, perhaps a ring of keys), always from his hand plus a real photograph; walls and cable are exact vector paths so they line up across pages. Smooth curves, never a jitter.
- No device on more than three slides. Slides 3, 4, 6, 10 and 12 stay plain evidence with the cable quiet at the bottom.

Loop 1 builds four key frames (slides 1, 3, 5 and 13) to judge the direction before the rest.
