# Reader round 11: projector and visual check

Seat: presentation designer and AV technician. Judged as the room will see it: a 3 m wide screen, the back row about 15 m away (five screen widths, which is the outer limit for comfortable reading), a 1024×768 projector with lifted blacks and a little blur. Material: all 46 pages at 1920×1080 (`.shots/deck/renovate-v2/`) and the twelve washed-out XGA sheets (`.shots/deck/projector/`). I judged from the PNGs and did not open the live page. The standing rules from LESSONS.md apply: no QR codes, plugs and keys stay simple one-line drawings, the closing socket plate stays as is, and "How this was made" is never a slide. Every fix below stays inside the current visual language.

## What holds up in the room

- **Type size is right almost everywhere.** At 1080 the titles are about 60 px, the body labels 30 to 36 px, and the qualifiers about 30 px. From 15 m on a 3 m screen, a 30 px line is roughly 4.5 cm tall, so it reads. Even after the washout, every title and every headline number on pages 1 to 41 can be read on the XGA sheets.
- **Hierarchy is clear, one point per page.** The title states the claim, and one number or one green element carries it: 40 against 32 (p. 7), 3.1 (p. 9), *renovate* (p. 13), 78% (p. 16), 4,000 (p. 21), 79% (p. 22), *checks first* (p. 25), 72% (p. 27), the green floor in the tower (pp. 28–36). On almost every page the eye lands first on the point.
- **The builds are in the right order.** Each new click adds the next comparison: US, then Germany, then since 2021 (pp. 23–25); timber, then duct, then renovate (pp. 10–13); one floor of the tower lit per step. None of the pages before a build looks broken.
- **The green has one meaning.** It marks the figure or state that matters and is never decoration. Dashed means planned and solid means built, and that reads without a legend.
- **Edge bars keep the bars readable.** A dark divider sits at the end of each fill, so a bar still reads as a proportion when its fill washes out (pp. 5–7, 16, 22, 27).
- **The bottom band is disciplined.** Only the cable, the source and the page marker sit in it, on every page.

## Consistency

- Titles, margins, the bottom band, the page markers and the green meaning are consistent across all 41 main pages.
- The cable behaves as LESSONS says: it rests in the band on the evidence slides and enters the drawing on the device slides (1, 5, 6, 7, 14, 16).
- The one grammar break is "running" drawn light grey on slide 11 (see the table above).
- Secondary text uses two greys (mid and light). After the washout, the light grey (axis labels, the year range on p. 21, "after 15 months", the Hall and Soskice line on p. 40) drops to about 3:1 contrast. Nothing essential is in light grey except the $278 million line and the 78% caveat (above).

## Embarrassment check

There is nothing that would embarrass the speaker. No text overflows or is clipped, no labels collide, and no page looks unfinished in the room. The closest call is p. 20, where a careful viewer could read "withdrawn" as attached to the July dot and ask about it.

## The three things that cost the most

1. **Pp. 31–33, the brick wall behind the channel washes out to almost nothing,** so the key metaphor vanishes on a real projector. Fix: render the drawing at the cable's ink (#2B2B2E), darker but not thicker.
2. **The mint and pale-grey fills are near-identical in brightness** (pp. 5–7, 16, 22, 27, B4), so green against grey fails on a weak projector or for colour-blind viewers. Fix: one step more saturation in the mint, or a green outline on green bars.
3. **P. 20, the Commonwealth Bank labels sit under the wrong dot and the axis end is unlabelled,** which invites a "which date is which?" question. Fix: align "Aug 2025: withdrawn" to the open dot and label or drop the ticks after 2025.
