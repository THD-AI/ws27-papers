## Notes by slide

** (page 1).** Deutsche Telekom openly takes an American result as its benchmark. On 5 October it told its investors about T-Mobile: software agents there now do a big part of customer service, and Telekom wants the same in Europe by 2030. My position: born in the United States, studying in Germany, so I may see Germany as the harder case. That's why every claim has a source. **Can Telekom copy what T-Mobile's agents did?**

**3 (page 3).** A correction to my own proposal. Telekom's release says AI agents now handle forty percent of customer contacts in the US; I used that number. In T-Mobile's own material I couldn't find it. Its slide counts something narrower: only contract customers, only contacts "served by an AI agent". Served means the customer meets the software first. Whether the software also finished the job, T-Mobile doesn't say. Company numbers, not audited. Still, one in three is a lot. **Telekom says forty percent. T-Mobile's own slide counts thirty-two percent.**

**5 (page 6).** My father is a civil engineer, and this picture is his. In America almost every new family home has a timber frame: the wall is hollow, so a new cable drops down inside it. In Germany it's mostly brick, built to stand, so a new cable means opening the wall. You can screw a duct onto the wall: quick, and the wall stays as it was. Or you cut a channel, lay the cable and plaster it over: slow, and the cable becomes part of the wall. The cable is the agent. The wall is what's already there: old systems, rules, people. The duct is repainting. Cutting into the wall is renovating. **The cable is the same. The wall decides the work.**

**7 (page 10).** Telekom isn't there yet. Across its European countries, a customer can prove who they are in fourteen different ways. Telekom wants three, and its own slide says why. **Simplify before automating.**

**13 (page 13).** So how would I do it? Step one: before any agent, measure what one contact costs today and how often customers come back; then, with the agent, how many requests it finishes for good and what it costs. That's the business measure from this course, not accuracy. If it doesn't get better, the agent is the wrong tool and Telekom stops there. Step two: sign the works agreement while the system is still on paper. Steps three and four: keep the option to switch suppliers, since about half of German firms with twenty or more staff depend strongly on American technology, and train the staff before each new key, measuring teams, never single people. **Measure first. Start beside the staff.**

**14 (page 16).** Step five. Fourteen ways of proving who you are become three, and the customer data becomes usable. An agent can't act on an account it can't identify. This is the channel cut into the brick. It takes years, and it can fail: in the third quarter of 2025 T-Mobile wrote off two hundred seventy-eight million dollars on a billing system it abandoned, about a tenth of its net income that quarter. **The biggest job is one customer record.**
