## Is this him?

Yes, more than any earlier round. The first five minutes are his shape: a situation before any number (slide 1), his bias said once in first person and turned into a reason for sources, the early short answer that the last line calls back, the correction to his own proposal, the long physical analogy from his father held for twelve sentences with the cable, the wall, the duct and the channel, and numbers turned into something a person can feel ("one in three", "sixteen every working day", "close to a tenth of that quarter's profit"). His grading survives: "Still, one in three is a lot", "That's not the same as solved. Still, a real result.", "Two cases don't make a rule." The slide-12 ladder (buy, screw on, can't cut it in) is Unit 6 exactly, with the break carrying the meaning. The closing callback ("I started out thinking Germany was the harder case. It is harder. That doesn't make it worse.") is the best three sentences in the talk and sound like a person, not a desk.

Where he thins out: the second-brick stretch (10 and 11) and the first half of 13 run five or six sourced sentences in a row with no "I" behind them. And three times he speaks to the grader instead of the room: "in this course's words" (2), "the business measure from this course" (13), "in the course's terms" (15). One is honest. Three is teaching up.

## Rhythm

His run-then-landing rhythm is there on most slides: sentences average 9 to 12 words, and every slide has short sentences in it. Two things flatten it.

**The landing often goes backwards.** The deck rule is "say the title at the last build". On 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 14 that works: the title is the verdict the slide was building to. On 3, 9, 12, 15 and 16 the note has already reached a better, warmer line of his own, and the bold title then steps back into a recap. Out loud you hear the energy drop on the very last sentence, which is the opposite of his Unit 8.

**"First" in every late landing.** "Here the checks come first" (11), "missing knowledge first" (12), "Measure first" (13), "read first" (15), plus "answer that first" in 10. By slide 13 the ear stops hearing the word. Not a rewrite of the titles, just a note: on 11 and 12 the speaker can lean on "checks" and "knowledge", not on "first".

## Slide by slide

**1.** Good opening: a scene, then where he stands, then why sources. "At T-Mobile, software agents now do a big part of customer service, and Telekom wants the same in Europe by 2030." is 21 words with a comma joint; fine at speed, a breath after "service". No change.

**2.** "My short answer: not the number, but the slow work behind it." Strong, and the last slide calls it back. The chatbot/agent scene is Unit 5 and lands. Problem: "For the company, in this course's words, that's a transformation, not just a change." comes between the scene and the landing, so "An agent doesn't just answer. It acts." arrives after a detour into course vocabulary. Fix: *"For the company, that's not a change. It's a transformation."* and drop "in this course's words" (the slide can carry the term), or move it before "A customer writes".

**3.** The correction is him. But the ear meets four sources in 30 s: Telekom's release, T-Mobile's own material, "even Telekom's investor slide shows thirty-two", then the landing "T-Mobile's own slide counts thirty-two percent". Is the 32 on Telekom's slide or T-Mobile's? The footnote says T-Mobile's AI Investor Day deck. A listener cannot resolve it, and the closing line ("can't import the 32%") depends on it. Fix (check the source first): *"In T-Mobile's own material I couldn't find the forty. Its investor slide shows thirty-two."* The landing then repeats what was said 25 s earlier; acceptable as the title read, but say it slower and drop the second "percent".

**4.** Clean. "Less than half." is a good two-word landing before the turn. No change.

**5.** The longest held analogy in the talk and it holds. "masonry" is a hard word for a non-native speaker and adds nothing after "brick and block": *"In Germany it's mostly brick and block, built to stand, so a new cable means opening the wall."* The cue sheet says build 3 fires on "cutting into the wall", but the note says "Or you cut a channel"; align the cue to "cut a channel" so he doesn't hunt for a phrase that isn't there.

**6.** Good pair: outside contractors on suppliers' systems / own engineers on own systems. "That's not the same as solved. Still, a real result." is his grade. "Their head of IT" after "T-Mobile didn't" is a number switch; spoken it's fine. No change.

**7.** Short and right; the sockets and plugs keep the house. "Telekom wants three, and its own slide says why." then "Simplify before automating." – make clear it's Telekom's line, not his: *"Its own slide says why: simplify before automating."* is one beat, then the title. Optional.

**8.** "From the street, repainting looks the same for a while" is a good callback. "In February 2024 Klarna stopped hiring and said its assistant did the work of seven hundred people in customer service, by its own estimate." is 24 words with the hedge hanging at the end, where the ear has already accepted the number. Sketch: *"In February 2024 Klarna stopped hiring. It said its assistant did the work of seven hundred people – its own estimate."* "the saving usually comes from the staff" is a claim from nowhere; one "I think" makes it his.

**9.** The strongest slide for him: ground (three bricks), the agreement, the routes, the number made physical, the pause, "I think that helps more than it hurts", then "That's what renovating means to me: the agent works with them, not instead of them." That is a landing. Then the bold "First brick: no dismissals, but positions go." pulls back to a fact. Fix: say the title as the claim right after the agreement (*"First brick: no dismissals, but positions go. People still leave, but only by agreed routes…"*) and end on "not instead of them". Tight window (65 s); the swap costs nothing. "ver.di": say "ver-DEE".

**10.** Six sentences, none short, no "I". "The EU asked people about digital monitoring at work. On AI and jobs, Germans are no more worried than Americans." To the ear, the EU survey asked Americans. Sketch: *"On AI and jobs, Germans worry about as much as Americans. On being watched at work, they don't. In the EU, only Sweden is more against it."* That also gives the slide a short sentence before "Any new system here has to answer that first."

**11.** "before launch" twice in two sentences ("answer that before launch… had no say before launch"). Fix: *"German law makes every new system answer that. At T-Mobile, as far as I could find, the staff had no say before launch."* "And here Telekom is ahead." is a good turn. "Results only in groups of more than five, with no names." is a fragment that works spoken.

**12.** "went to the firms… and asked: why not?" is a small scene, good. "Most of those firms are much smaller than Telekom. Still, the order says a lot." is concede-then-turn. The ladder ends on "you can't cut it in", the best spoken line in the second half. Then "Third brick: missing knowledge first. The law after." recaps the statistic he left two sentences ago. Fix: say the title after "each about six in ten" (*"…each about six in ten. Missing knowledge first, the law after."*), then "Most of those firms…", and end on "you can't cut it in."

**13.** "So how would I do it?" is his hinge question, mid-talk, answered at length: right. "If it doesn't get better" – "it" could be the cost, the measure or the agent; *"If those numbers don't get better…"*. "About half of German firms with twenty or more staff say they depend strongly on American technology." arrives cold, nothing calls it back, and it's on the slide; cut it from the notes (17 words). "Start beside the staff" is a coined phrase; fine on screen, but in the mouth "next to the staff" is what he would say.

**14.** Much better than round 11. "two hundred seventy-eight million dollars, before tax" – "before tax" is footnote material; drop it aloud. "This is the channel cut into the brick." is a good callback. Landing works.

**15.** The densest slide, at 11:17 to 12:33. "the model can ask, the system decides" and "It's how I'd give any new program access to real data: read first, then we see." are both his and both good. Then three things drag the last 20 s: "annex" (a written legal word; "an extra page of the works agreement" is what he'd say), the third course nod ("In the course's terms, that's a stage-gate model…"), and "The order is my hypothesis." (already on slides 13 and 15 as subtitle). Then the landing jumps back to step six. Fix: cut "The order is my hypothesis." and say the landing as a conclusion: *"…a works-council gate and a stop. So: read first, risky changes last."*

**16.** 182 words on one page, 84 s, 20 sentences. Two problems. The note opens with "Three things would prove me wrong." and says it again as the bold landing of build 3, 25 s later. Say it once, at the start, and let "Then the law weighs more than I said." close the list. And "I argue it from need" is academic phrasing nobody says aloud; *"My reason is simple: an agent can't act on an account it can't identify."* "Two economists, Hall and Soskice" – SOS-kiss. "…and mean it as a weakness" can go (the turn word "the other way" already carries it). From "People often say Germany is slow" to "That doesn't make it worse" it is concede, turn, land, exactly his shape. Keep all of that.
