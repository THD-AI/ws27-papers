## What I expected and what I got

I came in braced for "AI will transform customer service". What I got was a student who corrects his own number on slide 3, puts "company figures, not audited" in footers, draws a hatched box labelled "nothing published that I found" on slide 4 and ends with three ways he could be wrong. That earns a lot of trust in the first four minutes. Most AI talks I've sat through don't do any of that.

The trust leaks out later, and always on the same side: workers and customers.

## Fair to workers and customers, or cheerleading?

Mostly fair in tone, uneven in evidence.

- **The asymmetry (slides 6 and 8).** Klarna and Commonwealth Bank are told through their staff: 700 people, 45 roles. T-Mobile, the good example, is told through its systems: one customer record, 78% of chats, 74% help for staff. T-Mobile's $531 million of severance only appears in backup B1, row 10, and in prepared answer Q7. You say the filings don't tie it to the agents, and that's fair. But if I find that number on my phone during the talk and you didn't show it, I stop trusting slide 6. **Fix: one grey line on slide 6: "T-Mobile also booked $531 m severance in a 2026 restructuring; its filings don't tie it to the agents."**
- **Slide 6, 78% "never reached a person".** You add "isn't the same as resolved", which is good. From the customer's side there's a second reading you skip: some of those people wanted a person and gave up. The same goes for slide 4: contacts per account falling from 7.0 to 3.1 could mean better service or a harder road to a human. **Fix: say it on slide 4: "Fewer contacts can mean fewer problems, or customers giving up. The numbers don't tell which."**
- **Customers barely appear as people.** The tariff customer on slide 2 is the only one, and they never come back. There's no complaint, no satisfaction measure in the steps and no customer-side falsifier on slide 16.

## Is the worker thesis stated and backed?

It's **spoken but not shown, and only half backed.**

- "The agent works with them, not instead of them" is said twice in the notes (slides 9 and 16). No slide carries it. Slide 40, the line people photograph, talks about the 32% and "slow work". The worker thesis, which you say is the point, isn't on it. **Fix: put it on slide 40 under the green line: "The agent works with the staff, not instead of them."**
- The evidence for "with" is one T-Mobile figure (74% of conversations get a suggested next step, said aloud on slide 6 and shown only in B1). That shows assistance exists. It doesn't show that fewer people weren't needed. "With" and "fewer" can both be true at once, and to a call-centre worker that's the whole question.
- **The question I'd actually ask, missing from QUESTIONS.md:** when the bot takes the easy contacts, the people who remain get only the hard, angry and complicated ones. Per head, the job gets harder, not easier. That's what I saw on the phones. "Enabled by AI" has to answer that, and step 4 ("cases per hour ... per team") makes it worse, because cases per hour falls when only hard cases are left, and then the team looks slow. **Fix: on slide 13, add a staff-side measure to step 1 or step 4 ("load per person and sick days, before and with the agent") and prepare an answer to "do the people who stay get only the worst calls?"**

## Does it say what an agent's mistake costs a customer?

**No.** Slide 15 and B3 explain how mistakes are *limited* (read, undoable, fraud-prone, confirmed in the app, "the system decides"). That's good engineering, but it's the company's view. The cost is never named in the customer's own terms: moved to the wrong tariff, a contract term silently restarted for 24 months, a line cancelled, a bank account changed by a fraudster who talked the agent round, then two hours in a queue to undo it.

Prepared answer Q6 has the same gap. It repeats slide 15 and doesn't answer the question asked ("what does it cost *me*"). "There is always a way to a person" is a promise in your design, not something Telekom has said, and the room will hear it as a description of today.

**Fix: on slide 15, give one worked mistake in the customer's words, for example "Agent moves you to the wrong tariff: an 'undoable' key means one tap reverts it, at no cost, and a person calls back within a set time", and in Q6 say clearly that the way to a person is your requirement, not a current fact.**

## Is slide 9 honest?

**The headline is honest, the framing around it less so.**

Good: the title says "positions go", the chart shows real numbers (3,736 to 4,448), the totals are marked as yours, and Q4 says "Partly, yes". That's more honesty than I expected.

Problems:
1. **Scope blur.** The 4,000 a year are all of Telekom in Germany. The slide doesn't say how many were in customer service, and doesn't say none were caused by the agents. A listener can read the bars as "AI already costs 4,000 a year" or as "harmless background", and you steer neither way. **Fix: one line under the chart: "All of Telekom in Germany, all causes; how many were in service, and whether any were agents, isn't published."**
2. **"Any saving has to fit into that."** Said plainly, that means the agents' saving comes from not replacing people who leave. That's still replacement, just slower and kinder. Say it in those words, because the room will say it for you.
3. **"I think that helps more than it hurts"** is your opinion, placed right after the numbers, where it sounds like a conclusion. **Fix: either drop it or add "(my view)".**
4. **Who isn't covered.** A lot of German call-centre work is done at outside service providers. My friends who lost jobs weren't Telekom employees under the collective agreement. Q5 admits you didn't find what applies to contractors. That gap belongs on the slide, not only in Q&A. **Fix: add "Not covered: staff at outside service providers; nothing agreed after 2028" to the subtitle line.**
5. "Severance" listed among the "agreed routes" (notes) is still a way out of a job. Fine to say, but don't group it with retirement as if it were the same thing.

## Does the analogy help or hide?

**It helps for systems and hides for people.**

It works for one thing: why the same software costs more work in one place than another. The footnote "The walls picture conditions; they are not evidence" is exactly right.

It hides two things:
- **"The wall is ... people" (slide 5).** In the picture, the renovator cuts into the wall. If people are part of the wall, then people get cut into. You mean the opposite (Q8: "they decide how the cable goes in"), but the drawing says otherwise. **Fix: on slide 5, take people out of the wall and say who *lives in the house* during the work: "Staff and customers live in the house while it's renovated. They get the dust."**
- **Renovation is always the good option in the picture.** Nobody in the drawing pays for the dust and noise, and nobody asks whether the cable was needed at all. Step 1's stop rule is your answer, so connect them: "Sometimes the honest answer is no cable."

## Anything else that lowers trust

- Slide 2's "transformation, not just a change" and slide 40's Hall and Soskice close are the two places where it sounds like a course essay that has to finish on a positive note. "It is harder. That doesn't make it worse" is a nice line, but it's a verdict the talk hasn't fully earned for workers.
- Slide 11, US row: "no staff say found before launch" is absence of evidence drawn as a timeline. Keep "found", and say aloud that you looked only in public sources.
- Slide 16's three falsifiers are good, but all three are about Telekom's progress. None is about harm: repeat contacts, complaints or staff load going up. **Fix: make one falsifier "repeat contacts or complaints rise after a key goes live".**
- Title slide: "Drawings: generated from my own line drawing" is the right disclosure. Keep it.

## The three costliest items

1. **The worker thesis is only spoken, and its weak side (remaining staff get the hardest calls; the saving comes from not replacing leavers) is never faced.** Fix: put "works with the staff, not instead of them" on slide 40 and add a staff-load measure to step 4 on slide 13.
2. **The customer's cost of an agent's mistake is never named (slide 15, Q6).** Fix: one worked example on slide 15: wrong tariff, one-tap revert at no cost, a person within a set time, marked as your requirement.
3. **Bad examples are told through workers, the good one through systems (slides 6, 8, 9).** Fix: show T-Mobile's $531 m severance on slide 6 and the outsourced-staff gap on slide 9, each in one grey line.
