# Build specification

Two long-form articles by Alexander (Alex) Rudaev: one web page each, scroll-driven on screen, a two-column paper when printed. Static files, no framework, no bundler. Node 22 for the build, native ES modules in the browser.

The owner's bar, in his words: "not generic", "intuitive: people need to understand a visual immediately", "should not look made up", "at the level of a scientific research article", "needs to look like it was made by me", figures in the spirit of Processing: "scientific, simple, friendly". He likes scroll-driven storytelling. He rejects dark pages, fake hand-drawn filters, app-style badges and cards, generic tables and charts, and anything that reads as machine-made (one typeface everywhere, even strokes, perfect symmetry).

## Layout of the repository

```
SPEC.md
lib/build.mjs        builder: articles/<slug>/text.md -> dist/<slug>/index.html      (shell)
lib/article.css      page, type, tables, print                                          (shell)
lib/article.js       scroll engine, source marks, margin scenes                         (shell)
lib/fig.js           drawing helpers shared by all figures                              (figures)
tools/sources.mjs    builds articles/<slug>/sources.json from the research dossiers     (shell)
tools/shots.mjs      screenshots and PDF with puppeteer-core and the installed Chrome   (shell)
articles/<slug>/text.md, sources.json, data/*.json, figures/*.js, art/*.png
dist/                built site (ignored by git; published from the gh-pages branch later)
```

Slugs: `renovate` (Innovation) and `other-half` (Quantum).

## Text format (`text.md`)

Front matter: `title`, `deck`, `author`, `context`, `status`, `accent`. For search and sharing: `summary` (the meta description, under 160 characters), `date` (ISO), `keywords`, `card_alt` (the preview image's alt text), and optionally `draft: false` to allow indexing. `node tools/cards.mjs` writes the preview image to `art/card.png`. Then Markdown with these additions:

- `{{ID ID}}` at the end of a paragraph or list item: the ids of its sources in `sources.json`. Rendered as one small mark at the end of the passage. Selecting it opens the sources of that passage (title, publisher, link). In print the mark becomes bracketed numbers and a numbered list of sources is set at the end. Unknown ids fail the build.
- `:::scene <id>` … `:::` wraps passages that scroll beside one pinned figure. Inside, a line `@step n` sets the step shown while the following passages are in view. A line `@caption …` gives the caption used in print and on phones. `:::scene <id> web` draws the figure on screen only; print keeps its passages as plain text and doesn't number the figure. `@step n screen` marks a passage for the screen only: it says what the drawing does at that step, and print leaves it out (the caption carries it there). `:::scene <id> screen` is a whole scene for the screen only: print leaves out its figure and passages, and it takes a figure number on screen only (the figures after it carry both numbers, each shown in its medium). Where the scene reads as a sequence of stills (phones held sideways, small low windows, and upright windows where its figure cannot be pinned on top legibly in 45% of the height), a figure that exports `stills = [n]` with one step is shown once, at state n, after the first passage; with several steps, one still after the first passage of each step named; without the export, one before each step.
- `:::figure <id> <wide|column> [fold [summary]]` … `:::` a figure in the text flow; the block's text is its caption. With `fold` the drawing folds behind a switch on phones and wherever it is taller than the window, with the caption above the switch, closed and open (it never jumps below a drawing several windows long), for example `:::figure pairs wide fold Show the ten pairs` (closed it reads "Show the ten pairs", open "Hide the ten pairs"; without a summary, "Show figure n"). Opened by the reader, it is drawn whole at once, at the width of a wide figure (also in the appendix). Paper shows it whole. Where scenes stand beside their passages, a wide figure starts at the text column's left edge and reaches out to the right, as a scene does.
- `:::details <summary>` … `:::` supporting detail (a table, timings, a second check) folded behind a switch on screen, so the argument reads on; it is always open on paper. Closed it reads the summary, open "Hide the details".
- `:::steps [print]` … `:::` a numbered list drawn as a path of steps. `:::steps print` sets it on paper only (on screen a `:::scene <id> screen` tells the same).
- `:::margin <id> <state>` sets the state of the small margin scene from this point on. `:::margin <id> end` is where its story ends: on screen the pinned figure fades out as this point reaches it and does not come back; nothing is drawn there, on screen or on paper. `:::margin <id> off` sends it away for a stretch: it fades out as this point reaches it, as at `end`, and comes back over the 120 px before its next marker reaches the trigger line; nothing is drawn there either. While the margin figure is pinned, the page sets `--rail-away` on the root (0 shown, 1 gone) so that a figure taking over its picture can fade in as it fades out, with the scroll, both ways; without a pinned rail it is unset (read as 1).
- `@where <label>` on a line of its own sets the place the text is about (for example `United States · T-Mobile US`) from that point until the next `@where` or the next `##` heading. Rendered as a small mono kicker in the accent colour above the next block; inside a scene, above the first passage and wherever the place changes, and as a label at the top-left of the pinned figure that follows the active passage. Figures receive it as `ctx.where`.
- Tables are Markdown tables.

## Figure contract

Each figure is `articles/<slug>/figures/<id>.js`:

```js
export const steps = 5;                       // number of steps (1 for a plain figure)
export const aspect = { wide: 16 / 10, tall: 4 / 5 };
export const durations = { large: [2.4, 1.8, 2.0] };   // optional: seconds each step plays (by step number)
export default function mount(el, ctx) {      // el: empty container; build one <svg> in it
  // ctx = { layout: 'wide' | 'tall', print: boolean, reduced: boolean, data, css(name) }
  return { set(step, t) {}, destroy() {} };   // t in [0,1]: progress inside the step
}
```

- `set` must be idempotent and cheap: any `(step, t)` can be asked for in any order (scrolling backwards, jumping), and while a step plays it is called every frame. No timers that own state.
- `durations` (optional) gives the seconds each step plays over, indexed by step number, under the figure's size: `large` for a scene (clamped to 1.2–3 s), `medium` for a figure in the text (1–2.5 s), `small` for the margin scene (0.6–1.5 s). Without it: 1.8, 1.4 and 0.9 s. A figure should use its whole t range: what it leaves idle at the start of t is a pause after the trigger. A figure may run a gentle idle motion with `requestAnimationFrame` only while `ctx.reduced` and `ctx.print` are false and it is on screen; `set` decides what is true, idle motion only breathes.
- With `ctx.print` the shell calls `set(steps - 1, 1)` once. A figure whose steps show different information (not one picture assembling) draws a composite in print that contains all of it.
- `ctx.data` is `articles/<slug>/data/<id>.json` if present. Every number drawn comes from that file, and every entry there names its source id. No number is typed into drawing code.
- SVG only (prints sharp). `viewBox` set, width 100%. Text in SVG uses the page's fonts.
- The shell supports `?freeze=<id>:<step>:<t>` to pin any figure state for review, and `?print=1` to preview print mode on screen.

## Design system

- Page (changed 16 November: the first version, warm cream paper with a clay accent and a serif text face, was too close to Claude's own look, and the owner asked for it to go): paper `#FBFBFC`, ink `#15171C`, muted `#5C6370`, hairline `#DADDE3`. No grain, no cream, no clay, no terracotta, no orange anywhere.
- One accent per article, used for the one field and for emphasis only. `renovate`: line `#1F7A54`, field `#CDE9D9` (green). `other-half`: line `#4254C5`, field `#C9D3F7` (blue). Both lines pass 4.5:1 on the paper.
- Typefaces, the three of the owner's own CV, each with a job: Libre Franklin for body text, headings, captions and table text; Newsreader for the title, the deck and figure notes (notes in italic); JetBrains Mono for kickers, section numbers, axis numbers and units (tabular figures). No other family. Load from Google Fonts with `display=swap`.
- Any device, any orientation: the page must fit and read well from 320 px to 2560 px wide, in portrait and landscape, including phones held sideways (about 844 by 390) and tablets both ways. No sideways scrolling, no text under 11 px on screen inside figures, no pinned figure that leaves fewer than four lines of text visible.
- Kicker: small mono caps with `·` separators and a hairline that runs to the right edge, for example `INNOVATION MANAGEMENT FOR AI · WS 2026/27`. Section numbers in the accent colour.
- Screen: one text column of 62 to 68 characters, a right margin column of about 14rem for the margin scene and notes, wide figures and scenes break out. In a scene the figure is pinned (`position: sticky`) and the passages scroll past it. Three modes, the same conditions in `article.css` (2d) and `MODES` in `article.js`: **beside** the passages from 1000 px wide and 560 px high, and in short windows from 640 px wide and 450 to 559 px high (a low laptop window); **on top**, the figure pinned at the top of the screen and the passages scrolling under it, in windows under 1000 px wide and 560 px high or more (phones and tablets held upright): the drawing, measured as it is drawn and cut (its tall layout, cropped to what any step draws, not its frame), takes at most 45% of the window height, so at least 40% stays for the passages, and its lettering stays at 11.5 px or more; where the tall drawing cannot do both, the wide one is tried on windows from 640 px across, and where neither can, that scene is not pinned on this window but read as a sequence of stills (as below; `.scene.is-seq`), its text full width. Decided per scene and window size when the page is laid out (load, resize, turn of the device), from a copy drawn off screen once per width, never while scrolling; **stills** (the scene read as a sequence, each still fitted to 92% of the window height) in windows lower than 450 px (a phone held sideways) and in windows under 640 px wide and under 560 px high. The margin scene is pinned in the margin column from 900×420 (text 32.5rem + gap 3rem + margin 14rem + gutters fit in 840 px) and changes at each marker (see Motion); its opacity follows the distance to anything that crosses the margin column (gone 16 px before contact, faded over 120 px), so it never lies on content at any scroll speed. While scrolling, article.js first measures everything and then changes everything, so no frame lays out the page twice; the opening drawing's drift is a CSS scroll-driven animation where the browser supports it. Narrower or shorter windows show its states as stills at the markers, each playing from the state before as it comes into view. Two scenes in a row are not cross-faded: a figure stays in full ink while any of it is on screen and leaves with its caption under it; the next rises after that caption. A scene's first passage reaches the reading line once its figure stands whole in the window, half way to its pinned place. After a resize or a turn of the device, nothing is measured or played while the window changes; once it has settled (and the figures have redrawn at their new width), the page is brought back once: inside a scene the active passage is placed under the new layout's trigger line (below a figure pinned on top, not the middle of the window), so it stays the active passage; elsewhere the block that was at the reading line comes back to it, at the same point. Every figure is then set where it lands, at once, and the scroll events of that correction set figures at once too: nothing replays, no passage flips. Short hyphenated names of two parts, up to 14 characters ("T-Mobile", "J-curve"), do not break at the hyphen on screen (`.nobr`, set by the builder; not in links or code; paper breaks as before). A numbered list keeps the number given by its `start`.
- Print: A4, two columns, 15mm margins, 7mm gutter, body about 9.8pt, figures one column or full width, scenes as stills with their captions, margin scene states as small figures at their sections, running footer with title and page number. No grain, no sticky, no hidden text. Ctrl+P must give a paper.
- Tables: no header fill, no zebra. A 1pt ink rule above the header, a hairline under it, a 0.5pt rule at the end. Numbers in mono, right-aligned. Empty cells `·`.
- Figures, in the Processing spirit: structure in 1.25px ink strokes, data in 2 to 2.5px; quantities as counted units (one dot is one thing, stated in the figure); a faint dot grid behind measurement plates; notes in italic serif joined by thin leader lines; at most two pen-like marks per figure (a loose ring around the one number that matters). No filled badges, no ticks and crosses, no cards, no legends in boxes, no drop shadows, no gradients. Bars and counts start at zero. Motion moves forward: things assemble, units fill, a line draws from left to right; it is triggered by the scroll and played by time (see Motion). Seeded randomness only.
- Motion: the scroll decides which step, time decides how (17 November). A step scrubbed by the scroll lasts as long as the reader's scrolling: in 30–60 px a flick of the wheel ("bang, bang, bang", "jump scare"), in 320–400 px 10–30 s at reading pace, with the figure changing while the passage before was still being read. So:
  - **Trigger.** In a scene a step is due when the top of its first passage passes the trigger line, where the reader finishes the passage before it: beside a pinned figure 65% of the window (the reading line is at 50%, passages 40% apart); under a figure pinned on top 40% of a pitch below the reading line (on a 390×844 phone about 200 px under the figure, when the passage before has slid a third under the figure; before the figure is pinned the band starts at most at 80% of the window, so the first step is due once the figure is nearly whole). The first step is also due once 40% of the figure is in view, so it is drawn as it comes in (and a figure that fills a short window never scrolls in as an empty frame); before that, the figure shows its first step at t 0. Scrolling back past a trigger plays the step backwards. The active passage (full ink; is-read and is-current follow) and the place label change at the same moment, with the passages' 0.25 s colour transition.
  - **Play.** From what the figure shows to the step due, over time, eased (a prompt start at 1.4 times the mean speed, a gentle end at 0.6): a scene 1.8 s a step or its `durations.large` (1.2–3 s), but never longer than reading the step's own passage at 230 words a minute (a four-word passage gets 1.2 s); back 0.6 s a step. Several steps due at once (a fast flick) play one after another, quickly, each at least 0.5 s (the time divided between them); up to three behind, every one is played; more than three behind, the figure is set to the second-last and plays the last. A figure in the text plays once a quarter of it is in view (a quarter of the window for one taller than the window; or the page is at its end; at 40% a tall figure scrolled into a short window as an empty frame): 1.4 s or `durations.medium`; not again on the way back unless it left the window entirely. The margin scene plays 0.9 s (or `durations.small`) when its marker passes 65% of the window; its stills play like a figure in the text.
  - **Set at once.** A figure off screen (the margin scene while faded out), a jump (the contents, a link into the page, a new address, a resize or turn of the device, the first load), `prefers-reduced-motion` and print show their state complete at once. Stills of a scene read as a sequence are complete.
  - **Cost.** `requestAnimationFrame` runs only while a step plays; scroll and resize ask for one frame; a frame first measures (goals) and then writes (`set()` through `apply()`, which skips an unchanged state). The root carries `data-moving` while anything plays, and `window.__engine` (`players`, `finish()`, `duration()`) is there for the tools.
  - **Guarantee.** The figure shows the active passage's step complete within that step's time after the trigger. At reading pace a step completes before the reader is a quarter into its passage. `node tools/timing.mjs`, `tools/motion.mjs` and `tools/read.mjs` measure it (Quality checks).
- Every figure must pass this test: shown alone without caption for five seconds, a reader with no background says what it claims.
- En dashes, never em dashes. No emoji.

## Quality checks every builder runs before reporting

1. `node lib/build.mjs` runs clean; the page has no console errors.
2. Screenshots at 1440×900 at several scroll positions, at 390×844 and 360×800, and a PDF of the print version; look at them and fix overlaps, clipped text, widows in captions, labels touching lines.
3. No text smaller than 11px on screen or 6.5pt in print inside figures.
4. Keyboard: source marks reachable and closable with Escape. `prefers-reduced-motion` gives stills.
5. `node tools/motion.mjs <slug>` (1536×864, 390×844 and 360×800) flags no step: between triggers no drawing changes with the scroll; every triggered step plays by itself with the scroll held still, lasts 0.5 to 3.5 s of real time, is seen to change in at least 8 frames, and ends complete; a step played back lasts at least 0.3 s. `--frames <scene>[:<step>]` writes 12 frames over one step (on a clock the tool drives) and a contact sheet; still screenshots cannot show motion.
6. `node tools/timing.mjs` (both articles, 1536×864, 390×844 and 360×800, a wheel reader and a trackpad reader at 230 wpm, real time) flags no step: none under 0.5 s or over 3.5 s, none complete only after its passage is half read. `--video` records reading-pace videos into `.shots/video/`.
