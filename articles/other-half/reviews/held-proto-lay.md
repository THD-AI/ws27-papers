# Held scene prototype – lay reader

Reader: curious non-specialist, no physics, laptop and phone. First time seeing this figure.

## Step 1 – five-second test (held-1440x900-step4.png, written before anything else)

A tiny square grid next to a timeline of 8 rounds: a few checks light up at certain rounds (two together in round 3, one twice in rounds 5 and 6), and the lit ones get joined in pairs by dashed lines.

## Step 2 – reading it

**Desktop, mouse wheel (video frames).** The section text scrolls by normally, then the paragraph "While the computer runs ... a film in which a few points light up in every frame" arrives with the drawing beside it and stays. As I keep scrolling, numbered italic notes appear one by one under the paragraph, and the chart fills in: first one column of dots (round 1), then rounds 1 to 3 with the header 1 to 8, then the two blue boxes in round 3 together with the blue squares on the little chip, then z3 in rounds 5 and 6, then the dashed links. After step 5 the whole thing lets go and the text below ("The fired checks say roughly where...") comes up.

**Phone (390 wide).** Same order, stacked: paragraph, one note, title, chip, chart. Only the current note is shown; the earlier ones disappear. Everything fits on one screen, which is good, but I lose the list I had on the laptop.

**Ordinary (pinned) version.** The drawing is the same, but the notes float up one at a time in a mostly empty left column, with big gaps and half-cut notes at the top and bottom of the screen. The paragraph has already scrolled away by the time the lights appear.

## How far I got to the intended message

- The output is a series of rounds – yes, the column-by-column fill and the "round" header make that clear.
- Each round all 8 checks answer – yes, note 1 plus the 8 rows of dots.
- A flip on a qubit lights neighbouring checks in one round – mostly. I saw z0 and z1 light on the chip and in the chart at the same time. But "qubit 4" means nothing to me: no qubit has a number on the drawing, so I had to guess it is the dot with the little cross in the middle.
- A wrong report lights the same check in two rounds in a row – yes, once note 4 told me. The two boxes side by side in the z3 row are easy to see. Small worry: z3 also turns blue on the chip, which made me think something did happen there, even though the note says no qubit was hit.
- The decoder pairs lights across rounds, not round by round – I read the sentence, but the picture didn't prove it to me. The change in step 5 is just two short dashed lines inside boxes that were already lit, easy to miss. I couldn't see why "round by round" would go wrong, and "or to the edge" has nothing on the drawing to point at.

## Paragraph staying put while the drawing moved on

Natural. It felt like a slide with a presenter adding bullet points; I always knew which sentence the picture belonged to. The only odd moments were the empty stretch of white just before the scene starts and just after it ends, where I was scrolling and nothing seemed to happen. In frame 120 note 5 was already there, still grey, before the dashed lines showed up, so for a moment the text was ahead of the drawing.

## Held or ordinary

Held. The growing numbered list next to a still paragraph is calmer and easier to follow. The pinned version has notes drifting through empty space and the explaining paragraph is gone before the lights come on.

## What confused me

- "qubit 4" with no 4 anywhere on the drawing.
- z/x labels on the chip squares and the semicircles: the chart says flip-checks and phase-checks, the chip doesn't, so I didn't link them right away.
- Step 5's dashed links are faint, and the idea "not round by round" has no contrast shown.
- z3 lighting up on the chip during the wrong report.
- On the phone the earlier notes vanish, so by step 5 I can't look back at what step 3 said.

## Fixes (one line each)

1. Number the qubit dots on the chip (or at least label the crossed one "4") so the note matches the drawing.
2. Make step 5 visibly different: bolder pairing lines, and for a beat show the wrong round-by-round reading (e.g. z3 in round 5 alone, marked "?") before the across-round pair.
3. On the phone, keep earlier notes as dimmed lines like on the laptop instead of replacing them.
